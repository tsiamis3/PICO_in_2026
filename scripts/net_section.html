Network

The event time is reconstructed by a fully connected neural network with a heteroscedastic Gaussian output. The input is a 16-dimensional vector: for each of the four readout pads, the e-peak charge (qall), the e-peak amplitude (epeak), the signal maximum (vol_max) and the signal arrival time from a sigmoid fit (time_fit). Pads with charge below 1 are treated as empty: their charge and amplitude are set to 0 and their time to a fixed out-of-range value. Charges, amplitudes and voltages are min-max normalised per variable; times are used in ns without normalisation.

The network has five hidden layers of 32 ReLU units (≈4.8k parameters) and two outputs, the predicted time μ and log σ², so that every event gets its own time estimate and uncertainty. It is trained by minimising the Gaussian negative log-likelihood, L = ½[(t − μ)²/σ² + log σ² + log 2π], with Adam (learning rate 10−3), batch size 512, up to 1000 epochs and early stopping with patience 50, keeping the best weights.

Target

For each event a random offset ttrue ~ U(−1, 1) ns is added to all four pad times, and the network is trained to predict this offset from the shifted times and the pad amplitudes. The residual μ − ttrue therefore measures how precisely the network combines the pad information into one event time, including the charge-dependent (time-walk) and charge-sharing corrections it learns.

Training, validation and test

The data are shuffled and split with 10-fold cross-validation. In each fold, 90% of the events are used for training (of which 20% are held out for validation and early stopping) and the remaining 10% for testing, i.e. 72% / 18% / 10% per fold. Each event is tested exactly once, by the model that did not see it in training, so the results use the full data set (346k events nominal, 271k with 10 mV noise after removing incomplete events).

Three scenarios are studied: (A1) training and testing on nominal data; (A2) the ten nominal models applied to the noisy data, with the noisy amplitudes corrected for a per-pad voltage offset, normalised with the nominal ranges, and combined as an ensemble (σ² = mean predicted variance + variance of the ten predictions); (A3) training and testing on the noisy data with the same procedure as A1.

Performance metrics

The timing resolution is the width of μ − ttrue from a double-Gaussian fit, and the calibration of the predicted uncertainty is checked with the pull (μ − ttrue)/σ, which should have unit width. Both are studied as a function of the total charge and of the track position; events with |pull| > 5 are removed.

Inputs16 = 4 pads × (charge, e-peak amplitude, signal maximum, sigmoid time)
ArchitectureMLP, 5 × 32 ReLU, outputs μ and log σ²
LossGaussian negative log-likelihood (heteroscedastic)
OptimiserAdam, lr 10−3, batch 512, ≤ 1000 epochs, early stopping (patience 50)
Targetttrue ~ U(−1, 1) ns added to all pad times
